# 02 — Give me 8 minutes and I'll make you dangerously good with Claude

- **Format:** avatar to camera with screen-recording inserts, 16:9, about 8 minutes (~1,050 spoken words)
- **Title options:** "Give Me 8 Minutes and I'll Make You Dangerously Good With Claude" / "You're Using Claude Wrong. 6 Fixes in 8 Minutes"
- **Thumbnail text:** "STOP PROMPTING LIKE THIS"
- **Status:** draft. Needs screen recordings of Claude from your account for the `[SCREEN]` inserts; menu names should be checked against the app on the day.

`[MG: ...]` marks a motion graphic. `[SCREEN: ...]` marks a screen recording. `[SHORT n]` marks a section cut as a standalone vertical short.

## Script

**Hook**

Most people use Claude like a search box. They type one line, get an average answer, and decide AI is overrated.

`[MG: sad one-line prompt "write me a marketing plan" → grey, generic wall of text]`

The people getting ten times more out of it aren't smarter. They do six things differently. Here are all six, in eight minutes. Number five is the one almost nobody uses.

**[SHORT 1] One: give it the situation, not just the task.**

`[MG: split screen. Left "write a marketing plan". Right: same line plus "who I am / who it's for / what good looks like"]`

Claude can't see your life. So tell it three things. Who you are. Who this is for. And what a great result looks like.

Not "write a marketing plan". Instead: "I run a two-person bakery. This is for a launch in three weeks with a two-hundred-dollar budget. A great plan fits on one page and I can start it tomorrow."

Same tool. Completely different answer.

**[SHORT 2] Two: show it an example.**

`[SCREEN: pasting two past emails, then asking for a third in the same voice]`

If you want it to sound like you, don't describe your style. Paste two things you've written and say "match this". One example beats a paragraph of instructions, every time.

**[SHORT 3] Three: make it ask you questions first.**

`[MG: the line "Before you start, ask me anything you need to do this well." typed out, then highlighted]`

Add this one sentence to any big request: "Before you start, ask me anything you need to do this well."

Now instead of guessing, it interviews you. Five questions, two minutes of your time, and the first draft is already close to final.

**Four: stop starting from zero. Use Projects.**

`[SCREEN: opening a Project, showing uploaded files and custom instructions]`

If you explain your business in every new chat, you're wasting the best feature. A Project holds your files and your standing instructions. Brand voice, price list, customer profiles. Every chat inside it already knows them.

Set it up once. Then every conversation starts at step ten, not step one.

**[SHORT 4] Five: teach it your process once. That's a Skill.**

`[MG: a card labelled "Skill: Weekly report" with three steps, then it stamps onto three different chats]`

Here's the one most people miss. If you do something the same way every week, a report, a proposal, a content calendar, you can package that process as a Skill. Your steps, your format, your rules.

After that you just say "do the weekly report", and it follows your playbook, not its own guess.

Projects are what Claude knows. Skills are how Claude works.

**Six: connect it to your actual tools.**

`[SCREEN: connectors list, then Claude pulling a file from Drive and summarising it]`

Copy and paste is the slowest way to use AI. Connect Claude to where your work lives, your documents, your calendar, your notes, and ask it to go and get things itself.

"Find last quarter's sales sheet and tell me the three worst products" is one sentence. Without connectors, it's twenty minutes of your day.

**[SHORT 5] Bonus: argue with it.**

`[MG: draft → "What's weak about this?" → sharper draft]`

The first answer is a draft, not a verdict. Ask "what's weak about this?" Ask "what would an expert disagree with?" Ask for a version half as long.

The people who get great results aren't writing magic prompts. They're having a second and third turn.

**Close**

`[MG: six-item recap list, each ticked in turn]`

So. Give it the situation. Show an example. Let it ask questions. Keep your context in a Project. Turn repeat work into a Skill. Connect your tools. And always push back on the first draft.

Pick one of these and try it today. Then follow, because next week I'm handing Claude a real job for a full day and showing you everything that happens.
