# 01 — The Anthropic IPO, explained in 5 minutes

- **Format:** avatar to camera, 16:9, about 5 minutes (~720 spoken words)
- **Title options:** "Anthropic's IPO: The 4 Numbers Nobody Is Explaining" / "Anthropic Went Public. Here's What It Means If You Use Claude"
- **Thumbnail text:** "$107B. NOW WHAT?"
- **Status:** draft, facts checked against news coverage on 22 Nov 2026 (see Sources). Re-check the share price on the day of recording.

## Script

**[SHORT 1 — hook]**

Anthropic, the company behind Claude, just pulled off the biggest IPO in history. And within twenty-four hours, half the internet was calling it a bubble.

`[MG: ticker "ANT" slams in, price counter rolls 83 → 111.50 → 97.17]`

So who's right? Give me five minutes and four numbers, and you'll understand this better than most of the people shouting about it.

**Number one: one hundred and seven billion dollars.**

`[MG: big number "$107.3B", splits into "$81.3B IPO" + "$26B private placement"]`

That's how much Anthropic raised. Eighty-one billion from the IPO itself, plus twenty-six billion from a private placement. Nothing in market history comes close.

The shares were priced at eighty-three dollars. That's above the range they first marketed, because demand was so strong. On day one the stock opened at a hundred and eleven fifty, then slid, and closed at ninety-seven seventeen. Up seventeen percent.

`[MG: simple line chart of day one: open spike, slide, close; day two dips about 9%]`

The next day it dropped around nine percent. Still above the IPO price. But that open-high, close-lower shape is exactly why the word "bubble" started trending.

**[SHORT 2 — the growth vs the loss]**

**Number two: sixteen billion dollars.**

`[MG: bar chart, 2023 "$41M" tiny bar → H1 2026 "$16.3B" towering bar]`

That's Anthropic's revenue for just the first half of 2026. In all of 2023, it made forty-one million. Million, with an M. That is one of the fastest revenue ramps any company has ever reported.

This is the bull case in one chart. People aren't just trying Claude. Businesses are paying for it, and paying more every quarter.

**Number three: eighty billion dollars.**

`[MG: "$80.7B net loss" in red, then a smaller label: "operating loss: under $1B"]`

That's the reported net loss for the same six months. And this is the number the sceptics love.

But read it carefully. Coverage of the filing puts the operating loss, the cost of actually running the business, at well under a billion dollars. Most of that eighty billion comes from items outside day-to-day operations. So the headline is scarier than the business. It's still a number you can't ignore.

**[SHORT 3 — the real risk]**

**Number four: seven hundred and fifty billion dollars.**

`[MG: three cloud logos as plain labelled blocks: "Google $111B", "AWS $110B", "Microsoft $31B", stacking toward "$750B"]`

This is the one that actually matters. It's how much Anthropic says it could commit to compute. The chips and data centres that run Claude.

More than half of every dollar of revenue already goes to compute. And a big part of those commitments has to be paid whether customers show up or not.

Here's the twist. The companies renting Anthropic that compute are Google, Amazon and Microsoft. The same companies building their own AI to compete with it. Anthropic says so in its own risk factors: that capacity could be repriced or cut.

`[MG: split screen "BULL" vs "BEAR", two bullet points each]`

So here's the honest version. The bulls are right that the growth is real. The bears are right that the bill is enormous, and it's owed to rivals. The whole bet is whether revenue keeps outrunning the compute bill.

**[SHORT 4 — what it means for you]**

**So what changes if you just use Claude?**

`[MG: three-item checklist animates in]`

Three things. One: Anthropic now has over a hundred billion dollars to buy compute. If you've ever hit a usage limit, that's what this money is for. Two: public companies get judged every quarter. Expect more pressure on pricing and paid plans. Three: everything is now on the record. Every three months we get real numbers, not rumours.

I'll be reading those numbers so you don't have to. Follow for the next one. And none of this is financial advice. I'm here to explain it, not to tell you what to buy.
